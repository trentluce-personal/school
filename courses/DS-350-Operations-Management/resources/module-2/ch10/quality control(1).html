Process Control

  • SPC –Statistical Quality Control

Statistical evaluation of the output of a process during production of goods or delivery of service

  • SPC increases the Quality of Conformance

A product or service conforms to specification

Control Charts

  • Control Charts are the tools used to implement SPC
  • The general idea is to plot representative sample statistics (e.g. sample means) obtained from a process over time
  • Average (CL), Upper (UCL) and Lower control limits (LCL) define the range of acceptable variation

Concept of Variation in SPC

  • Random variation:

Natural variations in the output of a process, created by countless minor factors

  • Assignable variation (Non-Random):

A variation whose source can be identified

Control Chart Issues

  • Where in the process to use control charts
  • What size samples to take
  • What type of control chart to use
    • Variables–can measure even in fractions (length, weight, height, diameter, etc.)
    • Attributes–can be counted (pass/fail, defective/not defective, works/doesn’t work, good/bad, yes/no, operate/doesn’t operate, etc.)

Control Charts for Variables

  • Mean control charts
    • Used to monitor the average performance of the process (central tendency)
    • X bar chart (X bar = average or mean)
  • Range control charts
    • Used to monitor the process variation
    • R chart (R = range)

Control Chart for Attributes

  • p-Chart -Control chart used to monitor the proportion of defectives in a process
  • c-Chart -Control chart used to monitor the number of defects per unit

Process Capability

    • A comparison of control chart limits to design specification limits to determine if the process itself is (or is not) capable of making products within design specification (or tolerance) limits.
