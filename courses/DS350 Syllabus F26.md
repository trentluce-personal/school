## Course Description

Overview of the concepts, tools and techniques used in making managerial decisions related to the production or operations function of an organization. Topics include operations strategy, quality management and control, facility location and layout, forecasting, inventory planning, and others.

## Definition of a Credit Hour

Success in this 3-credit hour course is based on the expectation that students will spend, for each unit of credit, a minimum of 45 hours over the length of the course for instruction and preparation/studying or course related activities for a total of 135 hours. This means in a typical week, you would be expected to spend time on class activities, such as quizzes, homework, exams, studying, listening to audio files, practicing on problems, etc.

## Measurable Student Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

1. Define the different terms in Operations Management.
2. Describe the difference between Operations and Supply Chain management.
3. Identify the role of Operations in an organization and how it relates to other functions.
4. Review the different types of Operations and where they fit in the Product-Process matrix.
5. Distinguish between conceptual frameworks and decision-making quantitative models.
6. Describe the assumptions, inputs, and outputs of the different quantitative models.
7. Demonstrate the need to focus on the process, regardless if it is a product or service.
8. Solve problems using the right decision-making model and interpret the results.
9. Explain the difference(s) between optimal and heuristic (rule of thumb) solutions.

## Homework

There will be a total of 4 homework assignments (each worth 10 points). Each homework will be open for at least a week and will not be timed. The homework focuses on the analytical tools learnt in the chapters, i.e. require problem solving. Understanding the examples in the chapters, the solved problems at the end of the chapters, and listening to my VOICE OVER pdf files will help you do your homework. Homework alone is not meant to be comprehensive and reflective of what will be on the exam. I will give you the correct answers after the HW due date passes. I will recommend Practice Problems at the end of the chapters for you to solve on your own. These problems, along with all the examples and HW, represent the type of problems on the exams. I will post the solutions to these practice problems so you can compare and check your answers. You must complete all homework by the due date. If you miss any homework, there is no makeup and no extensions.

## Quizzes

There will be 7 quizzes (each worth 10 points) covering various chapters and only focusing on the concepts in the course (no problem solving). You can drop your lowest quiz, so only 6 total quizzes will count towards your grade. Each quiz will be open for at least a week and will be timed for 15 minutes. Each quiz is based only on the chapters assigned and will be a combination of 10 True/False and Multiple Choice questions. Reading the chapters and slides will help you learn the concepts and do well on the quizzes. You will be given the questions you missed after you submit your quiz. The last quiz, Quiz 7 (assessment), consists of 9 conceptual Multiple-Choice questions on all the chapters covered in the course. Each question is worth 1 point and you get 1 point bonus. Except for Quiz 7, I will post what you missed on a quiz and the correct answers after the due date passes. You must complete all quizzes by the due date. If you miss any Quiz, there is no makeup and no extensions.

## Exams

There will be a total of three exams during the semester, each worth 60 points, which must be taken by the due date. The exam will only include problems covered in the chapters. All exams must be completed only by the student enrolled in the course and without any help from any other student or source. Any detected misconduct will be severely punished with an F in the course for all students involved.

I will post a formula sheet on Blackboard prior to each exam. You can print it and use it in the exam. You must bring a calculator to the exam (required). Each exam is timed for 1 hour 30 min (90 minutes), so from the time you log in to take the exam, the clock starts ticking and you need to finish the exam within the time limit. So, while technically speaking the exam is open book open notes, there is no time to look up every problem i.e. it is not a take home exam. You must start the exam after you study the material and practice the problems. You will not be given what problems you missed on the exam and the correct answers after you submit it. If you would like to see what you missed, please contact me after the due date passes and the grades are posted. I will make each exam available for 3 days. This should allow you enough time to take an exam when it is convenient for you. I do not recommend you wait until the last minute to take the exam. If you miss an exam, there is no makeup.
