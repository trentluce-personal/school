# COURSE OVERVIEW

In this course you will prototype a new business. Under my careful supervision, you will perform various analyzes and receive continuous constructive feedback. The entire course revolves around a team case project and together we will make it a rich and challenging learning experience.

## Course Goal

Develop an original business idea and prepare an abridged business plan.

## Learning Outcomes

In this course, we will pursue together the following:

- Successfully perform analysis required to launch new businesses.
- Prototype businesses based on new venture ideas.
- Develop pitches for new venture ideas.

## Structure of the Course

The course consists primarily of lectures, supervised practice, and a team case project. We begin with an overview of entrepreneurship, its relevance and varieties. We turn next to a set of analysis required to launch new ventures, which involves examining (a) offerings available to consumers, (b) competition, and (c) the social and business environments. Moving on to prototyping, we look at the elements required to prototype a new venture idea. Then, we study how to develop a pitch to present new venture ideas to investors and business leaders. To ensure that we are able to apply the course content, we will work together on a team case project aimed at developing an original new business idea.

## Team case project (TCP)

This is the major project for the course (counting for 60% of the final grade). Before the second week of the semester, you will self-organize into five-person teams. Your team will develop a new venture idea and provide two different analyzes, a business plan, and a business pitch. Each of these deliverables, will be developed by the team members in the weeks indicated in the item schedule and reading list (below).

These deliverables will be prepared in PowerPoint, since this is a usual form of communication in business settings. You are free to create your own presentation structure, but I will provide some insights, and if necessary, a generic version to follow.

The purpose of this assignment is to incentive and support you to translate concepts into actionable practices, considering the content exposed in lectures and insights from the assigned readings. The basic content of each of the analyzes is as follows:

**TCP 1.** External Enablers: (a) Describe a historical process (past, present and future) of how your industry is changing over time. Then identify what are the emerging industry trends. (b) Present a list of insights that will be used to develop a new venture idea.

**TCP 2.** Competitive Landscape: Identify and group the attributes that drive purchase decision and describe the competitive landscape.

**TCP 3.** Business Plan: Outline your new venture idea and describe its positioning and business model.

**TCP 4.** Business Pitch: Create a digital version of your business pitch (mp4) to sell your business idea to investors and partners.

### How the TCP Sequence is Evaluated (Cumulative Work)

The TCP is designed as a progressive analysis that builds toward a launchable venture. Each stage incorporates the previous one; feedback from earlier TCPs must be integrated into later submissions. As noted below, TCP requirements are cumulative and unaddressed issues will continue to incur deductions in subsequent TCPs.

### Use of Artificial Intelligence in TCPs

To enrich the analytical quality of your TCP assignments, you are strongly encouraged to use artificial intelligence (AI) tools throughout your work. AI should be treated as an enabler to extend your capabilities, not as a substitute for your own reasoning.

Practical uses of AI include, but are not limited to:

- Research Support: Summarizing industry reports, identifying consumer trends, and exploring external enablers.
- Analytical Support: Structuring competitive landscapes, testing different frameworks, and running analyses.
- Creative Support: Refining positioning ideas, enhancing communication clarity, and stress-testing business models.
- Presentation Support: Improving data visualization, storytelling flow, and clarity of your business pitch.

# TEAM CASE PROJECT – DETAILED REQUIREMENTS

TCP assignments are graded based on the extent to which they meet the requirements below. Points may be lost on the following occasions.

- Minor errors (-1)
- Relevant errors (-3)
- Need for revision (-5)
- Missing sections or full revision required (-10)

## Cumulative & Data-Integrity Requirements for TCPs

- Cumulative submissions. TCP deliverables are progressive. Each new TCP must retain and refine all prior TCP slides (e.g., include TCP 1 inside TCP 2, TCP 1–2 inside TCP 3). This ensures a continuous venture story and allows prior feedback to be addressed.
- Address feedback. Requirements are cumulative. Unresolved issues from earlier TCPs carry forward and continue to affect your grade in later TCPs (see grading notes).
- Attachments, not links. Upload your PowerPoint (.pptx) and, when required, your Excel (.xlsx) directly to Blackboard. External links may break, require access, or be modified after the deadline and will not be accepted for grading.
- Excel required for charts. Any competitive landscape or chart used to support an analysis must be data-driven and verifiable. Submit the Excel file that feeds the chart, and ensure the chart in your slide deck matches the spreadsheet 1:1 (no redraws).

## TCP 1: External Enablers

Consumer and market trends (1-2 slides) – 3G analysis

- Past, present, and future of the industry in which the business operates or aims to operate.

- Specific names for industry moments
- Evidence to support claims
- Logos of companies operating at every stage of the industry.

External Environment (1 slide)

- Identification of insights for new venture ideas for each of the 8 dimensions of analysis (e.g., Economic, Sociocultural, Global, Technological, Political/Legal, Ecological, Demographic).

### Problem (1 slide) – Optional Slide

- Description of the problem in a way that a layperson will understand.
- Why the problem matters and what people gain from solving it.

### Gap in the marketplace (1 slide) – Optional Slide

- Description of the market gap in a way that a layperson will understand.
- Why the gap matters and what people gain from filling it.

## TCP 2: Competitive Landscape

Drivers of consumption (1 slide)

- Presentation of a coherent and realistic analysis containing all product attributes that drive the consumption in the industry in which the business you are creating aims to operate (e.g., quality, price, etc.)
  - The analysis should converge to two emotional drivers of consumption (see PPT).
- Assignment of percentage values for each attribute that drives the consumption in the

industry.

Competitive landscape (1 slide)

- Data-driven competitive landscape chart reflecting the extent to which each company

performs on both emotional attributes.

- The analysis must include a comprehensive sample of competitors, covering direct competitors (those offering similar products/services), indirect competitors (alternative solutions to the same need), and potential entrants (emerging models or technologies that could reshape the market).
  - Sample size: The number of competitors should be sufficient to reflect the full structure of the market. A limited sample may distort results, conceal market saturation or niche opportunities, and reduce the analytical credibility of your findings. Each chart should present enough data points to convey a realistic and evidence-based understanding of the competitive field.

Competitive groups (1 slide - Overlay)

- Identification of competitive groups
  - Names for each group

### Notes:

TCP 2 submission requires attaching an Excel spreadsheet that shows the competitive landscape chart is based on your data and that your calculations are correct.

The spreadsheet provided in the Course Materials folder is a template for reference only—a placeholder example to illustrate format, not scope. Each group must customize and expand the spreadsheet to reflect the realities of their chosen market and the full range of competitors analyzed.

trajectory.
