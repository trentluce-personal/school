## Course Description

This course provides a comprehensive introduction to modern database management systems, covering the complete spectrum from fundamental data modeling concepts to advanced analytics and big data technologies. Students will master the database development lifecycle including conceptual modeling with Entity-Relationship diagrams, logical design using the relational model, physical implementation, and database administration. The course emphasizes both transactional and analytical systems, reflecting current industry demands for professionals who understand traditional database design as well as modern data integration, warehousing, and analytics platforms. Students will gain hands-on experience with SQL programming, database design tools, and contemporary technologies including NoSQL databases, cloud-based solutions, and data virtualization platforms.

## Measurable Student Learning Outcomes

### Undergraduate Level

Upon successful completion of this course, undergraduate students will be able to:

- Design and Model: Create comprehensive Entity-Relationship models and convert them to normalized relational database designs using industry-standard techniques.
- Implement and Query: Write complex SQL queries including advanced analytical functions (ROW_NUMBER, RANK, GROUPING SETS, CUBE, ROLLUP) for data retrieval, manipulation, and analysis.
- Apply Technology: Utilize modern database technologies including cloud-based solutions, NoSQL databases (MongoDB), and data integration platforms.
- Optimize Performance: Apply physical database design principles including indexing strategies and performance tuning techniques.
- Integrate Systems: Understand the role of databases in multi-tier applications and implement database connectivity in programming environments.

### Graduate Level

Upon successful completion of this course, graduate students will be able to:

- Architect Solutions: Design enterprise-level database architectures that integrate transactional and analytical systems using modern data platforms and virtualization technologies.
- Synthesize Advanced Concepts: Integrate big data technologies, data warehousing, and analytics platforms to create comprehensive data management solutions.
- Evaluate Strategic Impact: Assess the organizational implications of database design decisions on business intelligence, analytics capabilities, and competitive advantage.
- Lead Implementation: Develop and manage complex database projects incorporating data governance, quality management, and master data management principles.
- Drive Innovation: Apply emerging technologies including AI-enhanced database systems, data fabric architectures, and real-time analytics to solve contemporary business challenges.

## Assignments

Total course points are distributed across four categories: Exams (50%, 300 points), Homework Assignments (30%, 180 points), In-Class Exercises (15%, 90 points), and Participation and Attendance (5%, 30 points).

### Undergraduate and Graduate Requirements

MIS 600 enrolls both undergraduate and graduate students. Both groups follow the same grading structure: the same four categories, the same weights, and the same point totals. The difference is the scope of work inside each category. Graduate students complete additional or extended components matched to the graduate learning outcomes, so both groups are graded out of the same totals while graduate students produce more work at a higher level.

- Chapter quizzes and matching exercises (5 points each): identical for both groups.
- Written assignments (8 points each): undergraduate students respond to the core prompt (about one page). Graduate students complete the core prompt plus a graduate extension of roughly one additional page connecting the chapter to enterprise architecture, data governance, or strategic impact, citing at least one scholarly or practitioner source (maps to Evaluate Strategic Impact and Synthesize Advanced Concepts).
- Exams (100 points each): the undergraduate exam consists of the objective section. The graduate exam contains the same objective section plus one integrative scenario question, with point weights adjusted so both versions total 100 points (maps to Architect Solutions and Lead Implementation).
- class exercises (9 points each): each exercise includes a designated graduate extension step (maps to Drive Innovation).

Examples of graduate extensions: on the Chapter 2 written assignment, graduate students also identify one data governance or data quality implication of their design; on the Chapter 6 in-class exercise, graduate students also write one analytic query using ROW_NUMBER or RANK with a brief note on its indexing implications; on the Chapter 9 written assignment, graduate students also recommend a high-level data architecture and its business intelligence payoff.

### Exams (50% · 300 points total)

Three exams at 100 points each (300 points). Only your highest three exam scores count toward the final grade. Exam 2 is mandatory; missing it counts as missing two exams, since SQL proficiency is essential to the rest of the course.

- Exam 1 (Sep 23): Chapters 1-4 (Database Environment and Modeling).
- Exam 2 (Oct 21): Chapters 5-6 (SQL). Mandatory.
- Exam 3 (Nov 18): Chapters 8, 9, 11, and 13 (Physical Design and Advanced Topics).
- Final Exam (Dec 5-10): Comprehensive and optional; it can replace your lowest exam score.
- Graduate versions of each exam include one additional integrative scenario question, with point weights adjusted so both versions total 100 points.

### Homework Assignments (30% · 180 points total)

Ten chapters at 18 points each (180 points). Each chapter includes a quiz (5 points, multiple choice), a matching exercise (5 points, terminology), and a written assignment (8 points, database implications). Graduate students complete the graduate extension on each written assignment as described above.

- The top 8 scores in each category (quiz, matching, written) count toward the final grade.
- Students receive 2 drops in each category to accommodate emergencies.

### In-Class Exercises (15% · 90 points total)

- Ten exercises at 9 points each; the top 8 scores count toward the final grade. The Week 1 asynchronous Draw.io ERD Starter counts as In-Class Exercise 1 and is submitted on Blackboard.
- Participation and completion based, focused on practical database applications.
- Cannot be made up if absent.

## Week 1 Asynchronous Assignment: Draw.io ERD Starter (9 points)

There is no in-person meeting on Wednesday, August 19. Before our first in-person class on August 26, complete two tasks from home or campus:

1. Read this syllabus in full. Your submission must end with this statement: "I have read and understood the MIS 600 syllabus, including the grading structure, the automatic drop policy, and the no-late-work policy."
2. Learn the basics of drawing an Entity-Relationship diagram in Draw.io (app.diagrams.net), the free tool we use all semester. Work through the Draw.io ER diagram guide (https://drawio-app.com/blog/entity-relationship-diagrams-with-draw-io/) or the video walkthrough by Dr. Soper (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lAtCySGDD48), then build a simple ERD for this mini-case: a university department offers courses, each course has sections, and students enroll in sections.

- Undergraduate students: model at least three entities (for example, STUDENT, COURSE, SECTION) with a primary key and at least two attributes per entity, and connect them with labeled relationships.
- Graduate students: model at least five entities, including one associative entity (for example, ENROLLMENT carrying a grade attribute), and add a short paragraph (100-150 words) explaining one design decision you made and its consequence for the database.

3. Submit on Blackboard. Export your diagram as PDF or PNG and submit it by Tuesday, August 25, at 11:59 PM.

This assignment is graded for completion and good-faith effort, not modeling correctness. We will formally cover ER modeling in Chapters 2 and 3; the goal this week is that you arrive on August 26 already comfortable opening Draw.io and producing a diagram.
